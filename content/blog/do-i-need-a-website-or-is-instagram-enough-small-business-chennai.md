If people already find you through Instagram and you only need chats and bookings through DMs, Instagram can carry you for a while. ## What Instagram is good at

Instagram is good for being seen by people who were not looking for you. A Reel can put you in front of strangers in a way a website never will. It is also free to start, fast to change and easy to use from a phone, which is why most small businesses begin there.

Use it for:

- **Reach.** Showing your work, your face and your way of thinking to people who do not know you yet.
- **Warmth.** Regular posts make a stranger feel they know you before they message.
- **Quick conversations.** DMs and replies are the fastest way to answer a lead.

## What Instagram is bad at

This is where businesses get stuck.

1. **You do not own it.** The reach, the account and the rules belong to someone else. A change in the algorithm, or a locked account, and your shop window is gone.
2. **Google barely sees it.** Someone searching "counsellor in Chennai" or "uniform manufacturer in Chennai" is far more likely to land on a website than on a profile.
3. **It cannot hold a long explanation.** Prices, process, the answers to the same ten questions, booking details. On Instagram you repeat them in DMs forever. On a website you write them once.
4. **It does not feel official to everyone.** Some customers, especially companies and parents, want to see a proper website before they trust you with money.

## What a website is good at

A website works while you sleep. It shows up when people search, answers the repeated questions, and gives you one link you can put everywhere: your Instagram bio, your LinkedIn, your visiting card, your WhatsApp.

It is the place to put:

- What you do, in plain words
- Who you do it for
- Proof: real work and real reviews
- How to contact or book you
- Your prices or at least how pricing works

## The comparison, side by side

- **Cost to start:** Instagram is free. A website has a one time build cost plus a yearly domain and hosting fee.
- **Finding new people:** Instagram finds them through content. A website finds them through Google searches.
- **Ownership:** you do not own Instagram. You own your website.
- **Long answers:** Instagram cannot hold them. A website can.
- **Bookings:** awkward on Instagram. Simple on a website.
- **Trust with cautious buyers:** some on Instagram. A lot on a website.

## Which one first?

Here is how I decide with a business owner, in order.

**If you already get customers through DMs and word of mouth:** keep posting, and build a simple website next. You do not need ten pages. One clear page with who you are, what you do, proof and a way to contact you is a good start.

**If you have nothing yet:** start with a basic website and one social channel, not five. A website without any posting is quiet, and posting without a website sends people nowhere. Pick the channel where your customers already are. For founders and consultants that is often LinkedIn. For local services and creators it is often Instagram.

**If your customers are companies, parents or anyone who checks you out before paying:** website first. They will look you up.

**If you are a very early idea and want to test demand:** Instagram or LinkedIn first, and a simple page later. Do not spend on a website to test an idea you have not said out loud yet.

## The mistakes I see most

- **Building the website and never posting.** A website with no new content goes quiet and Google has little reason to show it.
- **Posting for months with no website.** Every interested person hits a dead end and you answer the same questions in DMs.
- **Paying for a huge site.** A small business does not need twenty pages and a custom app. It needs a clear home page, a services page, an about page, proof and a contact route.
- **Not owning the domain.** Make sure the domain is registered in your name. Ask who owns it before you pay.
- **Treating the website like a brochure.** It should answer the questions customers actually ask, in their words.

## A simple setup that works for most small businesses

1. A domain in your name.
2. A WordPress website with a home page, services, about, a few real examples and a contact or booking page.
3. One social channel you can keep up, linking back to the website.
4. A Google Business Profile if you serve a local area, so you show up on Google Maps.
5. One piece of content a week that answers a real customer question, posted on the site and the social channel.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Instagram enough for a small business?**
Sometimes, for a while. If your customers find you through Instagram and you take orders through DMs, it can work. It stops being enough when you want to be found on Google, look more established, or stop repeating the same answers.

**How much does a website cost in Chennai?**
It depends on the size, whether it has booking or a shop, and who builds it. I will not quote a number here because a fair price depends on your scope. Ask for a total in writing that includes the domain, hosting, number of pages, number of changes and any yearly costs, as I explain in [the WordPress checklist](/blog/wordpress-website-chennai-what-to-ask).

**Can I build the website myself?**
Yes, with tools like WordPress, Wix or Canva sites. It is a fine start if you have time. Hire someone when your time is worth more than the fee, or when you need booking, SEO or a more careful design.

**Do I still need social media if I have a website?**
Yes, because a website does not bring strangers by itself. Social content, plus Google search, is how people reach it.

**How long before a new website shows up on Google?**
Weeks to months. A new site needs to be indexed and to earn trust. Submitting it to Google Search Console and publishing useful pages helps.

**What should my first website page say?**
Who you help, what you do, why they should trust you, and what to do next. Say it the way a customer would ask the question.
