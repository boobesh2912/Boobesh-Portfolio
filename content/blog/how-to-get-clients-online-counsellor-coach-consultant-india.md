**The short answer:** to get clients online as a counsellor, coach or consultant, you need four things in order: a clear sentence about who you help, a simple website that proves you are real, one social channel where you share useful content regularly, and a way for interested people to contact or book you in under a minute. Most people do the second and third without the first, which is why their posts get likes and no enquiries.

## Step 1: say who you help in one sentence

This is the step most people skip, and it is why content feels hard.

Weak: "I am a psychologist and I help people with mental health."
Better: "I help college students deal with exam anxiety through weekly online sessions."

The second one tells a stranger whether you are for them. You are not narrowing your business, you are making it easy to refer. People send clients to someone whose job they can repeat in one line.

Write three versions and read them aloud. Choose the one a friend could repeat to someone else.

## Step 2: build a small website that proves you are real

For a service person, a website has a different job from a shop. It answers the question "can I trust this person with my problem?"

A good small website has:

- **A home page** with your one-sentence promise and a clear next step.
- **An about page** with your face, your background and your qualifications, written like a human.
- **A services page** that says what you offer and how a session works.
- **Proof.** Reviews, testimonials or work samples, with real names where people agree.
- **A way to book or contact you.** For some of my clients that is online booking, because it removes the back and forth.

A website you cannot update goes stale, and a stale website looks like a closed shop.

## Step 3: pick one channel and use it every week

You do not need to be everywhere. Pick the channel where your clients already spend time.

- **LinkedIn** suits consultants, coaches for professionals, and anyone whose clients are working adults or companies. Read [LinkedIn personal branding for founders](/blog/linkedin-personal-branding-for-founders).
- **Instagram** suits counsellors, wellness, creators and anyone whose clients discover people through short videos.
- **A newsletter** suits you if you like writing and want people to hear from you without an algorithm in between. I wrote about it in [how to start a newsletter as a student](/blog/how-to-start-a-newsletter-as-a-student-in-india), and the ideas apply to professionals too.

Consistency beats volume. One useful post a week for six months beats thirty posts in one burst and then silence.

## Step 4: post things that answer real questions

Content that gets clients is not "motivational quotes". It is the answers to the questions people have before they hire you.

For a psychologist or counsellor, that might be:

- "What happens in a first counselling session?"
- "How do I know if I need therapy or just a break?"
- "What does confidentiality mean in practice?"

For a coach or consultant:

- "What does a coach actually do in the first month?"
- "How do I know if a coach is right for me?"
- "What results are realistic, and in what time?"

Write the question as the headline and answer it in plain words. This works on a blog, in a LinkedIn post, and as the script for a Reel. One answer becomes three pieces of content. I explain the repurposing idea in [content marketing strategy for startups](/blog/content-marketing-strategy-for-startups) and the script structure in the [Instagram Reels script framework](/blog/instagram-reels-script-framework).

A note for counsellors and health professionals: be careful with claims. Do not promise cures or guaranteed results, and follow the ethical rules of your profession. Honest content also builds more trust than big promises.

## Step 5: make contacting you effortless

I have seen good profiles lose clients to one small problem: nobody could tell how to book. Fix these:

- A single link in your Instagram and LinkedIn bio that goes to your website or booking page.
- One WhatsApp or booking button on every page.
- A reply within a day, even if it is only "I will get back to you tomorrow".
- A clear first step, like a free introductory call, if that suits your practice.

## Common mistakes

1. **Starting with the logo and colours.** Brand colours matter far less than a clear promise.
2. **Posting without a call to action.** Every post should have one small next step.
3. **Copying a famous creator's style.** Their audience came from their story, not their font.
4. **Waiting to feel ready.** You will never feel ready. Publish the imperfect first version.
5. **No proof.** Ask happy clients for a short review and permission to quote it. Reviews do more than any caption.
6. **Spreading across five platforms.** One channel done well beats five done badly.

## A 30 day plan you can start this week

**Week 1:** write your one-sentence promise, collect your qualifications and two or three reviews, choose your channel.
**Week 2:** publish a simple website with home, about, services and contact or booking.
**Week 3:** post three useful answers to real client questions, on your chosen channel and as short pieces on your site.
**Week 4:** ask two past clients for reviews, share your website link everywhere, and check what got the most response so you can do more of it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do counsellors and coaches need a website?**
It is not mandatory, but it helps a lot. Clients who are about to share something personal want to see a real, professional presence first, and Google tends to show websites ahead of social profiles.

**Which social platform is best for coaches?**
The one your clients use. For professionals and companies it is usually LinkedIn. For individuals looking for wellness or personal support it is often Instagram.

**How long does it take to get the first client online?**
It varies a lot. Some people get a first enquiry within weeks, usually through people who already know them. Treat the first few months as building a base, not a promise of a number.

**Should I run ads?**
Maybe later. Get the website, the message and a few reviews right first. Ads send people to your website, and a weak website wastes the money.

**Can I do this myself?**
Yes. Everything here can be done on your own. Hire help when your time is worth more than the fee, or when you want it done faster and more carefully.
