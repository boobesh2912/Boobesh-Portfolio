**The short answer:** in the first 30 days a good marketer in Chennai should agree the goal and the numbers with you in week one, publish something real by week two, show you what the early content is doing by week three, and by week four tell you plainly what to keep, change and drop. If you reach day 30 with only a "strategy deck", something is off.

I am a content marketer in Chennai and I run [Gari Tech](/gari-tech), so I am biased. Use this as a checklist for whoever you hire, including me. If you have not hired anyone yet, start with [how to hire a content marketer in Chennai](/blog/how-to-hire-a-content-marketer-in-chennai).

## Week 1: agree what success is

Before any content is made, you and your marketer should agree on three things in writing.

1. **The goal.** More enquiries, more followers who are actually your customers, more signups. Pick one.
2. **Two or three numbers to track.** Not ten. Enquiries, website visits from content, replies to your posts. Pick numbers you can check yourself.
3. **The scope.** What gets delivered, how many changes you can ask for, and who approves things.

This is the step most people skip. When I led marketing at Tribe Fortis, we never set up a proper KPI tracker, and it is the main thing I would do differently. I wrote about it in the [Tribe Fortis case study](/blog/tribe-fortis-marketing-case-study).

## Week 2: something real goes live

By the end of the second week there should be actual content published, even if it is small. A few posts, a Reel, a first newsletter. A calendar for the month should exist too, and it should survive a busy week. If you want to build one yourself, see [content calendars out of spreadsheets](/blog/content-calendars-out-of-spreadsheets).

Look at the quality of the thinking, not only the design. Does it sound like your business? Does each piece have a clear point for one clear reader?

## Week 3: look at what happened

Now there is something to measure. Ask for a short update that covers:

- What was published
- Which pieces got a response, and which did not
- One thing learned

Early numbers will be small. That is normal. What matters is whether your marketer can explain them honestly and adjust. If the update is all screenshots of likes and no explanation, ask harder questions. A useful rule of thumb is a mix of roughly 80% value and 20% selling in what you publish, and week three is a good time to check you are not selling too hard or too little.

## Week 4: keep, change, drop

At the end of the month you should get a plain answer to three questions:

1. What is working, and should we do more of it?
2. What is not, and should we stop?
3. What is the plan for next month?

A good marketer will also tell you what they would change about their own work. If everything was perfect, they are not looking closely enough.

## Red flags in the first month

- Guaranteed followers or viral reach. Nobody honest can promise it.
- Nothing published by day 14.
- No agreed numbers, so nothing can be judged.
- You cannot tell who is actually writing your content.
- Reports that are all vanity numbers and no explanation.
