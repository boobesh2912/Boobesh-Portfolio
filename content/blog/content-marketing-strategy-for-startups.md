**The short answer:** a startup content strategy is one clear customer, one clear message, two channels, a weekly rhythm you can actually keep, and a monthly look at what worked. Everything else is decoration. Here are the seven steps I use, and give to clients of [Gari Tech](/gari-tech).

## 1. Pick one customer

Not "small businesses". A specific person: a counsellor starting a private practice, a college student choosing a career, a gym owner in Tambaram. The narrower you are, the easier every later decision becomes.

## 2. Write the one thing you want them to believe

One sentence. For example: "Learning to code is easier if you build something you care about." If you cannot write it, no amount of posting will fix that, because you are not yet sure what you are saying.

## 3. Choose two channels, not six

Go where your customer already spends time. For founders and professionals that is often LinkedIn. For consumer brands it is often Instagram. Add a newsletter or YouTube later. Two channels done consistently beat six done for a month.

I stayed consistent on one platform, LinkedIn, for a long time, and that consistency taught me more than any tactic. More on that in [LinkedIn personal branding for founders](/blog/linkedin-personal-branding-for-founders).

## 4. Choose three content themes

Themes, not topics. For a fitness brand it might be "the people", "the workouts" and "myths we get asked about". Every post fits one theme. This stops you from staring at a blank page each Monday.

## 5. Build a calendar you can keep

Decide the smallest rhythm you will not skip. Two posts a week that actually go out beat five that fade by March. Plan a few weeks ahead, not a few months.

## 6. Reuse everything

One long video or article can become a handful of posts, a short clip and a newsletter section. Making one idea travel is the cheapest growth habit there is. It is most of what I do for Your College Senior.

## 7. Review monthly, in writing

Once a month, list which posts got replies, saves, shares or enquiries. Write two sentences on what they had in common. Make more of that next month. This takes twenty minutes and most teams skip it.

## Mistakes I see most often

- Starting with a channel, not a customer.
- Posting when there is something to say, which in practice means rarely.
- Measuring followers instead of enquiries.
- Quitting in week three, right before it starts to work.

That last one is personal. I have more than 50 unfinished projects on my drive. Content marketing punishes that habit harder than almost anything else.

## A worked example, step by step

This is an illustration, not a real client. Imagine a small counselling practice that wants more bookings.

1. **Customer:** working adults in Chennai who feel stressed and do not know whether counselling is for them.
2. **Belief:** "Asking for help is normal, and the first session is easier than you think."
3. **Channels:** Instagram for reach, and a simple website with a booking form for the decision.
4. **Themes:** "what a first session is like", "myths about counselling", "small habits that help".
5. **Calendar:** two posts a week and one short video, planned four weeks ahead.
6. **Reuse:** each video becomes a post, and each month the best posts are collected into one article on the website.
7. **Review:** each month, count bookings that mention the content, and see which topics got saved or shared.

Nothing here is clever. It is clear, and it is repeatable.

## A template for content pillars

Pick three or four, and give each a job.

- **Teach:** how-tos and explanations that build authority.
- **Prove:** case studies, reviews and results that build trust.
- **Show:** behind the scenes and people, which build familiarity.
- **Debunk:** myths and mistakes, which earn comments and shares.

Then check every idea against the list. If it fits none, skip it.

## A template for an editorial calendar

Keep it to a few columns: date, channel, pillar, idea, format, status, and the result after publishing. The last column is the one people skip, and it is the one that makes the plan better each month.

## A distribution checklist

Publishing is not the same as distributing. For each piece, ask:

- Did I share it where my customers actually are?
- Did I post it natively on each platform rather than only pasting a link?
- Did I reply to the first comments quickly?
- Did I send it to two or three people who would care?
- Did I link to it from another page on my website?

## Metrics that matter, and ones that mislead

- **Matter:** enquiries, bookings, sales conversations, replies, newsletter sign-ups, saves and shares.
- **Mislead:** raw views, follower counts, and likes on their own.

Track a small set and review it monthly. A long dashboard nobody reads is worse than four numbers you act on.

## A simple 90 day plan

- **Days 1 to 30:** decide the customer and message, set up the profile and website, publish your first eight to ten pieces.
- **Days 31 to 60:** keep the rhythm, reply to everyone, and collect the first patterns in what works.
- **Days 61 to 90:** double down on the best two themes, drop the weakest, and write your first case study or testimonial post.

## Tools that are enough

You can run a small content operation with very little: a notes app for ideas, a spreadsheet or board for the calendar, Canva for design, a phone for video, and a simple analytics view on each platform. Adding tools will not fix a missing strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a content marketing strategy?

A written plan for who you are speaking to, what you will say, where you will publish it and how you will measure whether it works.

### How do I create a content marketing strategy for a startup?

Follow the seven steps above: pick one customer, write one belief, choose two channels, choose three themes, build a calendar you can keep, reuse everything and review monthly.

### How much content should a startup publish?

The most you can keep up without dropping quality. For most small teams that is two to three pieces a week on one or two channels.

### How long before content marketing works for a startup?

Give it about three months of consistent work before judging, and longer for search traffic.
