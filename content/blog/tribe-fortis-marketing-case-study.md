**The short version:** from June to September 2026 I was Marketing Lead at Tribe Fortis, a fitness and wellness brand in Chennai. I led a small team and wrote a lot of content. I also never set up a way to measure any of it, so I cannot honestly tell you it worked. This is the case study I would want to read before hiring someone, which means it includes the part that did not go well.

I am [Boobesh AG](/about), and I run [Gari Tech](/gari-tech), a content marketing agency in Chennai. I wrote this on my own account, and it says nothing about Tribe Fortis beyond my own work there.

## The brand

Tribe Fortis runs fitness and wellness programs across three audiences: corporate wellness for companies (B2B), fitness for adults (B2C), and programs for kids. It is built around a simple idea, which is that training should feel like a game. That is a good problem for a marketer, and a tricky one, because the same brand has to talk to a company, an adult and a parent.

## How I came in

I took over marketing at the end of May 2026 from the outgoing lead. The founder gave me clear authority to drive the positioning and the content direction, which is not something every marketing hire gets, and I was grateful for it.

## What I did

- **Led a five person team.** A videographer, editors, and designers who made the carousels.
- **Wrote Instagram Reels scripts across three verticals:** corporate wellness, adult fitness and kids. Each audience needs a different hook and tone.
- **Wrote the brand's LinkedIn content.** I also ran a live LinkedIn profile session for one of my teammates, which is the same kind of work I do in my [LinkedIn sessions for students](/blog/why-your-linkedin-is-not-getting-you-internships).
- **Changed the content mix.** The feed leaned heavily promotional. I pushed it toward roughly 80% value and 20% selling, on the theory that people trust a brand that teaches before it asks.
- **Helped plan and scope brand events,** including a networking collaboration with a founders' community.
- **Kept a shared content pipeline moving,** so scripts, edits and designs reached the right people in the right order.

## What fell short

This is the part most case studies skip.

**I did not see a meaningful lift in reach or awareness.** That is my honest read of it. But I need to be careful, because of the next point.

**No KPI or measurement system was ever set up.** There is no follower, impression or engagement tracker to point to, so I cannot prove that the work moved things, and I cannot prove that it did not. "It did not do well" is an impression, not a result, and that is a failure of mine as the lead.

**The team ran on ad hoc coordination.** Reviews and quick calls on WhatsApp and Meet, with no written process and no regular reporting rhythm.

## How it ended

The engagement wound down on 30 September 2026. I do not want to speak for the founder's decisions, so I will say only what I think about my own side. My results were not strong enough, and not clearly measured enough, to make me an obvious person to keep. I was paid for being embedded and available, not for a stated outcome, and that is a weak position to be in when anyone asks what the money is buying.

## What I learned

1. **A role is not an offer.** I worked as a role, meaning paid for time and general output, rather than against a clear outcome. If you cannot say in one sentence what is supposed to be different in three months, you are vulnerable.
2. **Every engagement gets a tracker on day one.** Not a dashboard, a simple tracker. Without it you are arguing from feelings. From here, I am treating this as a rule for anything new I take on.
3. **Specific, provable results beat being available.** Someone who does everything and shows what it produced is easier to keep than someone who does everything and cannot show anything.
4. **Work for a client does not build your own name by default.** Four months of content went onto Tribe Fortis's channels and nothing compounded for my own portfolio. Writing this case study is partly me fixing that.

Ask them what they would measure in the first week. If they do not have an answer, you are about to repeat what I just described.
