**The short answer:** to start freelancing as a student in India, pick one skill you can already do, offer one small piece of work to a community of people who might need it, get one real client, finish the job properly, then ask for the next one. I did exactly this in my first year of college in Chennai, and my first paying client came from a WhatsApp group, not from a platform.

I am Boobesh, aka Boo. I started [Gari Tech](/gari-tech) in February 2024, was nominated for [Best Student Freelancer at the Global Freelancers Festival 2026](/blog/nominated-best-student-freelancer-global-freelancers-festival-2026), and have made plenty of mistakes along the way. This is the honest version.

## Is freelancing in college actually realistic?

Yes, but slow and small at the start. My first website paid me 1,500 rupees. That is not a business, that is a beginning. If someone tells you a student can make lakhs in month one, they are selling you something.

What college gives you is time to be bad at it cheaply. Nobody is depending on your income, so you can afford to learn on real work.

## Pick one skill, not five

I started with Canva, in my first year, because it was the one thing I could do that someone would pay for. Then I added websites. The full story, including the part where I said I knew WordPress before I had ever opened it, is in [how I started Gari Tech](/blog/how-i-started-gari-tech-student-entrepreneur-chennai).

You do not need to start with your dream service. Start with the thing you can already do, then grow.

## How I got my first client with zero portfolio

My first client is Aldo Einsty, a psychologist and the founder of Mindful Mindvoice.

I did not have a single sample to show her. What I had was a free offer. I posted in a Chennai freelancers WhatsApp group that I would build a free website. She replied.

Once we talked about what she actually needed, it was clearly more than what a free offer could cover, and I told her so. We agreed on a price. It was low, even by the standards of that time. She still trusted me, with no portfolio, no reviews and nothing to look at, and I am really grateful for that. She is the reason the rest of this happened.

I built the website. She liked it a lot. My first payment from her was 1,500 rupees for the site.

It did not stop there. She later became my social media management client. For her Instagram page I wrote 3 Reels scripts, edited 2 videos and created 20 posts.

Here is what I took from it:

1. **A small free offer to a community is a very good first move.** It costs you only time, and it lets someone say yes without risk.
2. **Be honest about the limit of free.** When the work was bigger than free, I said so and we agreed a price, instead of quietly doing more for nothing.
3. **One happy client can become two kinds of client.** A website turned into ongoing social media work, because the first job went well.

Please do not copy the 1,500 rupees. I undercharged. Copy the part where we agreed a price before the work.

## Build a portfolio when you have no clients

Your first client becomes your first portfolio piece, which is why finishing matters more than starting. Every site or design you complete is proof for the next person. That is why I keep my live work and the client reviews on the [Gari Tech page](/gari-tech).

If you offer content work, here is a tip I give people: write three evergreen pieces and post them. Evergreen means useful next year as much as today. Three real pieces on your own page say more than any "I am passionate about content" line.

## Where to find your first client

I will only tell you what I know. My first client came from a WhatsApp community of Chennai freelancers. Since then, most of my clients have come through referrals and trials.

Platforms like Upwork, Fiverr and Internshala are often listed in guides for students. I did not start there, so I cannot give you a first hand review of them. Try them if they suit you, but also tell the people around you, join communities of people who hire freelancers, and make a small offer.

## The client I should have said no to

I will not say who, and I will not say what industry. She does not deserve to be a lesson on the internet.

I took the project because I had no other client. The price was low again. And then came the pressure: every tiny alignment, every minute change, again and again. It made me anxious about work I should have been proud of.

I do not regret it, because I learned a lot. I learned how to choose a client, and how small things at the start lead to a whole path later. If you only have one client, it is tempting to say yes to everything. Do not say yes to everything.

What I do now:

- **Agree the price, the scope and the number of changes before you start.** Not after.
- **Notice the first week.** The way a client behaves in the first week is usually the way they will behave all project.
- **Having no other client is not a reason to give up your peace.** There will be another one.

## Freelancing alongside exams and attendance

I do not have a magic timetable for you, and I am not going to invent one. What I follow is simple: be clear with clients about when you can reply, never promise something you cannot deliver, and promise less than you can do so you can be early. I wrote more about balancing college and a business in my [founder story](/blog/how-i-started-gari-tech-student-entrepreneur-chennai).

## Taxes and paperwork

I am not a chartered accountant, so I will not give tax advice. What I will say: freelance income can be taxable in India, and the rules change. Read the official pages for [income tax](https://www.incometax.gov.in) and [GST](https://www.gst.gov.in), or ask a chartered accountant, before your income grows.

## Red flags that mean slow down

These come from my own experience, so take them as opinion:

- No clear scope, just "make it nice".
- Unlimited changes with no end.
- Pressure to start before the price is agreed.
- A client who is already stressing you out in week one.

## Questions students ask me

### Can a college student freelance in India?

Yes. I started freelancing in my first year of college. Check the tax and paperwork side as your income grows.

### Do I need a portfolio before I start?

No. I got my first client with nothing to show. Build the portfolio from that first project, and consider writing three evergreen pieces if you offer content work.

### How do I find my first client?

Mine came from a free website offer in a Chennai freelancers WhatsApp group. Offer something small and clear to a community of people who might need it, and tell the people you know.

### How much should I charge?

I charged 1,500 rupees for my first website, which was too little. Agree the price, scope and number of changes before you start, and do not take the first price anyone offers just because you are new.

### Should I work for free?

A small free offer to get your first client can work, like it did for me. But say clearly where free ends, and agree a price for anything beyond it.
