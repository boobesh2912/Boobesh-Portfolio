**The short version:** during the lockdown, when I was in 10th standard, I started reselling kitchen utilities on ShareChat. Containers, oil dispensers, nut organisers. I used the tablet my parents had bought me for online classes. The money was small, I spent it badly, and it is still the most useful thing I ever did, because it was my first taste of selling.

I am Boobesh, aka Boo, a content marketer in Chennai and the founder of [Gari Tech](/gari-tech). People ask how I got into business. This is the real beginning, and it is not glamorous.

## The tablet that was not only for classes

Corona time. Everybody was at home, all day, all the time. My parents bought me a tablet for online study, and I used it for a lot more than study. I watched videos about programming, I kept seeing EdTech ads everywhere, and one day I typed in the thing almost every student types at some point: *how to make money online*.

I did not become rich that day. But that search is where everything else starts, including the [agency I run now](/blog/how-i-started-gari-tech-student-entrepreneur-chennai).

## The idea came from my mom's phone

Here is the part I like most. I did not have a clever market research method. I just looked around my own house.

My mom used ShareChat a lot at that time. Everybody was at home, so everybody was on their phones. And when I started digging, I noticed that kitchen utilities were selling well on there, mostly to people like my mom, who were very active on the platform.

So the thought was simple: post pictures of kitchen products on ShareChat, put my phone number next to them, and see if anyone writes.

What I sold:

- Containers
- Oil dispensers
- Nut organisers

Not exciting. Very practical. That is probably why it worked.

## The one lesson I use even now

I did not invent a new product or a new market. I found where the buyers already were and showed up with something useful. That is basically what I do for clients today, only now it is called content marketing.

Most small businesses I meet do the opposite. They build the thing first and then go looking for people. It is almost always easier to ask: where are my customers already spending time, and what are they already looking for?

I learned that from my mom scrolling ShareChat. I should probably tell her that.

## The money, and what I did with it

The money was small. I want to be honest about that, because the internet is full of people who say they made lakhs in their first month, and I did not.

The first thing I did with it was buy five pairs of sunglasses. Five. Different colours. I still do not know why. There was no plan. I was a teenager with my own money for the first time and I wanted five of something.

Looking back, I wasted that money. And I also kept trying lots of other things around the same time, jumping from one idea to the next without finishing anything properly. If you want a sneak preview of the rest of my life, that is it.

I do not regret it. Wasting a small amount of money at 15 is a cheap way to learn what not to do with a bigger amount later. I would just tell younger me to keep a little aside before buying the fifth pair.

## What my parents said

They have always supported me in whatever I do. My parents rarely say no when it is about learning, whether that was the tablet, later a laptop, or later hosting and a domain for my first website. I know not everyone has that, and I do not take it for granted. It is a big reason I could try things at all.

If you are a parent reading this: the thing that helped me was not money. It was that nobody laughed when I said I wanted to try something odd.

## If you want to start small too

You do not need a big idea. Here is the simplest version of what I did:

1. **Look at where people around you already spend time.** For me it was ShareChat on my mom's phone.
2. **Pick something people already want and use every day.** Boring and useful beats clever.
3. **Show it clearly and make it easy to contact you.** Pictures and a phone number were enough.
4. **Keep your first money separate from your fun money.** Or buy five pairs of sunglasses and learn the hard way, like me.
5. **Write down what you learned.** The profit was small. The lesson was not.
