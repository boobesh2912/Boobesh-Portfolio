**The short answer:** content marketing is the thinking and the material, meaning what you say, to whom, and in what form. Social media marketing is running your accounts on platforms like Instagram and LinkedIn. Content marketing feeds social media, and it also feeds your website, your emails and search.

## Side by side

- **Purpose.** Content marketing builds trust over time. Social media marketing builds presence and conversation on specific platforms.
- **Where it lives.** Content lives everywhere: a blog, a newsletter, a video, a podcast, a landing page. Social media lives on the platforms.
- **Time horizon.** Content compounds over months. A social post has a short life, hours to a couple of days.
- **Skills.** Content needs strategy and writing. Social media adds community management, timing and design.

## An example

Say you run a counselling centre. Your content marketing might be a series of plain explanations: what a first session looks like, how to tell whether you need help, what confidentiality means. Those become articles on your website, a newsletter, and short videos.

Your social media marketing takes pieces of that and posts them on Instagram and LinkedIn, replies to comments and messages, and keeps the accounts alive.

Without the content, the social accounts have nothing worth saying. Without the social accounts, the content may never be seen. You need both, in that order.

## Which one first?

- If you have **no message yet**, start with content strategy. Posting more will not fix a blurry message.
- If you have **good material and no audience**, focus on distribution and social media.
- If you need **sales this week**, look at paid ads, and let content and social do the slow work in the background.

## Why agencies blur the two

Many agencies are really social media agencies that also call themselves content marketing agencies, because it sounds bigger. The test is simple: ask who decides what gets said. If the answer is "you send us the topics", it is social media management. If the answer includes strategy, research and a plan, it is content marketing. Neither is wrong, but pay for what you are getting.
