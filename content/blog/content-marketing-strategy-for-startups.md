**The short answer:** a startup content strategy is one clear customer, one clear message, two channels, a weekly rhythm you can actually keep, and a monthly look at what worked. Everything else is decoration. Here are the seven steps I use, and give to clients of [Gari Tech](/gari-tech).

## 1. Pick one customer

Not "small businesses". A specific person: a counsellor starting a private practice, a college student choosing a career, a gym owner in Tambaram. The narrower you are, the easier every later decision becomes.

## 2. Write the one thing you want them to believe

One sentence. For example: "Learning to code is easier if you build something you care about." If you cannot write it, no amount of posting will fix that, because you are not yet sure what you are saying.

## 3. Choose two channels, not six

Go where your customer already spends time. For founders and professionals that is often LinkedIn. For consumer brands it is often Instagram. Add a newsletter or YouTube later. Two channels done consistently beat six done for a month.

I stayed consistent on one platform, LinkedIn, for a long time, and that consistency taught me more than any tactic. More on that in [LinkedIn personal branding for founders](/blog/linkedin-personal-branding-for-founders).

## 4. Choose three content themes

Themes, not topics. For a fitness brand it might be "the people", "the workouts" and "myths we get asked about". Every post fits one theme. This stops you from staring at a blank page each Monday.

## 5. Build a calendar you can keep

Decide the smallest rhythm you will not skip. Two posts a week that actually go out beat five that fade by March. Plan a few weeks ahead, not a few months.

## 6. Reuse everything

One long video or article can become a handful of posts, a short clip and a newsletter section. Making one idea travel is the cheapest growth habit there is. It is most of what I do for Your College Senior.

## 7. Review monthly, in writing

Once a month, list which posts got replies, saves, shares or enquiries. Write two sentences on what they had in common. Make more of that next month. This takes twenty minutes and most teams skip it.

## Mistakes I see most often

- Starting with a channel, not a customer.
- Posting when there is something to say, which in practice means rarely.
- Measuring followers instead of enquiries.
- Quitting in week three, right before it starts to work.

That last one is personal. I have more than 50 unfinished projects on my drive. Content marketing punishes that habit harder than almost anything else.

Money, not much, if you do it yourself. Time, a lot.
