**Content marketing is creating useful things, such as articles, videos, posts and newsletters, that your future customers want to consume, so they trust you before they ever buy.** You are paying with information and attention instead of paying for an ad slot.

## A simple way to see it

An advert says "buy this". Content says "here is something useful", and the buying decision comes later, once you have earned it.

When I was 15 I sold things online without knowing any of this. I just knew people bought when you said it the right way, and that is content marketing at its smallest.

## The main types

- **Written:** blog articles, LinkedIn posts, newsletters.
- **Video:** YouTube, Instagram Reels, short clips cut from longer videos.
- **Audio:** podcasts.
- **Visual:** posters, carousels, infographics.
- **Tools and resources:** calculators, templates, checklists.

You do not need all of them. Pick the one your customers already spend time on.

## How it differs from other marketing

- **Advertising** buys attention for as long as you keep paying. Content keeps working after you stop, but starts slowly.
- **SEO** is about being found on search engines. Content is what you get found with. They are partners, not rivals.
- **Social media marketing** is running your accounts. Content is what you put on them. The difference is covered in [content marketing vs social media marketing](/blog/content-marketing-vs-social-media-marketing).

## How it actually works

1. A stranger finds something useful you made.
2. They come back, or follow you, because it was useful again.
3. After a few of these, they know who you are and what you are good at.
4. When they need what you sell, you are the obvious name.

The slow part is steps 2 and 3. Most people stop before they get there.

## A real example of repurposing

At Your College Senior, the channel has more than 150 videos and around 8,700 subscribers at the time of writing. A big part of my job there is taking one long video and turning it into LinkedIn posts, short clips and a newsletter, so the same idea reaches people in three places without being made three times. That habit, making one idea travel, is worth more than making more ideas.

## How to start this week

1. Write down the three questions your customers ask most.
2. Answer each one in a short post or video.
3. Publish one a week for eight weeks. Do not judge the results before that.
4. Keep the ones that got replies. Make more like them.

For a fuller plan, read [content marketing strategy for startups](/blog/content-marketing-strategy-for-startups).

## How to know if it is working

Look at things that matter to a business: enquiries, bookings, replies, people who mention your content when they contact you. Views and likes are nice, and easy to inflate.
