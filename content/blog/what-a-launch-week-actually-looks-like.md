Launch week always looks clean from the outside. A countdown post, a big reveal, a graph going up and to the right. From the inside it looks like six people in a group chat at eleven at night arguing about whether a comma changes the meaning of a headline. Both things are true at once.

## monday: the calm before

Everything is scheduled. Everyone has reviewed the assets twice. This is the day you convince yourself the plan is airtight, right up until someone asks what happens if the landing page gets more traffic than the server expects. You did not think about that. Now you are thinking about that.

## tuesday: the actual launch

The post goes live. For the first hour you refresh the analytics dashboard more than is healthy. Numbers move, then they stall, then a much bigger account shares it and everything spikes at once. Somewhere in here the dashboard lags for twenty minutes and nobody notices because everyone is too busy replying to comments in real time.

## wednesday: the correction

The first day's numbers tell you what actually landed, and it is rarely the piece you expected. The quiet email got more replies than the loud post. You adjust the rest of the week's content on the fly instead of sticking to the original plan, because the plan was a guess and the data is not.

## thursday and friday: the long tail

This is where most of the actual results happen, quietly, after the excitement has worn off. Slower conversions, people who saw it Tuesday and came back Thursday ready to act. This is also where most campaigns get abandoned too early, because the team has already moved on emotionally.

## the marketer's job in all of this

Stay calm on Monday, stay fast on Tuesday, stay honest on Wednesday and stay present through Friday. The highlight reel only shows Tuesday. The actual work is the other four days.
