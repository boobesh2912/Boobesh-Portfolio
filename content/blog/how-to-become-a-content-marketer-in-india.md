**The short answer:** to become a content marketer in India with no experience, learn to write and tell stories, post your own work in public, build a small portfolio from three real pieces, then get a first client or internship by showing that portfolio. You do not need a marketing degree. I have a B.Tech in Computer Science and Business Systems, and I run content for a living.

I am Boobesh, aka Boo, an entrepreneur from Chennai and the founder of [Gari Tech](/gari-tech). I will not pretend this is a formula. It is one person's path, including the parts that did not work.

## What a content marketer actually does

On a normal day it is some mix of these: deciding what a brand should say, planning a content calendar, writing Instagram Reels scripts, writing LinkedIn posts and newsletters, turning one long video into a week of content, and working with editors and designers so things actually get published.

I do this at Your College Senior, for StoryIt and for Proof, and I did it as Marketing Lead at Tribe Fortis, where I led a five person team. If you want the basics first, start with [what content marketing is](/blog/what-is-content-marketing).

## Content writer, content marketer, social media manager

They overlap, so here is the simple way I think about them:

- **Content writer:** writes the words.
- **Content marketer:** decides what to say, to whom, in what order, and why, then makes sure it gets made.
- **Social media manager:** runs the accounts, posts, replies, tracks what happens.

I wrote more on the last two in [content marketing vs social media marketing](/blog/content-marketing-vs-social-media-marketing). In small teams, one person does all three, and that is fine.

## Skill number one: write like you talk

My first LinkedIn posts were written with AI. I got no engagement, and I understood why later. LinkedIn is a place where you should act naturally, and if the post is AI, it is not you. So I started writing everything myself.

Now my posts are my own experience and nothing else. I document what I did, where I went, what I tried and what failed. If someone wants to know who Boobesh is, they can open my LinkedIn and read. The tone is conversational, close to how I talk.

I get ideas from conversations, not from sitting down to think "what should I post". A friend once told me I know a bit about many things but nothing deeply. I turned that into a post.

## A simple post structure

Mine is hook, body, call to action:

1. **Hook:** the first line has to make your reader stop scrolling. I write for two groups, entrepreneurs and students, so I write the hook for whoever I am talking to.
2. **Body:** short lines. Leave a blank line after every two lines. Nobody wants a huge paragraph, so keep it breathable.
3. **CTA:** end with something that makes people reply in the comments.

That is it. Do not overthink the rest.

## Build a portfolio from nothing

Write three evergreen pieces and post them. Evergreen means useful next year as much as today. Three real pieces, on a page people can open, say more than "I am passionate about content".

Here is a trick I borrowed from freelancing. Pick a local business you can find on Google Maps, make a sample of what you would do for them, like a month of post ideas, and send it. If they like it, you have a client. If they do not, you still have a finished piece of work. I explained the same idea for web work in [how to start freelancing as a student](/blog/how-to-start-freelancing-as-a-student-in-india).

## Your first client or role

My path was not a straight line. I started reselling online at 15. In first year of college I did freelancing and design, mostly posters, then added the services people actually wanted and that became Gari Tech in February 2024. The content roles came after that.

Your first one can be a friend's business, a college club, a small local shop or an internship. If you apply for internships, do not pay anyone to get one. An internship is for experience, not for a certificate.

## What nobody tells you: measure from day one

At Tribe Fortis I led content for four months, and I could not prove what the work did, because no one set up a way to measure it. No KPIs, no numbers to point to, so I cannot tell you honestly whether it worked. It is the biggest lesson of my career so far, and I wrote it all down in the [Tribe Fortis case study](/blog/tribe-fortis-marketing-case-study).

So when you take your first client, decide on day one what you will track, even if it is only three numbers.

## A few honest notes

- You will be bad at first. Everyone is.
- Your first posts will not get views. Keep going past week two.
- Learn from people who do the work, not only from people who sell courses about it.

## Questions people ask me

### Do I need a degree to become a content marketer?

No. A marketing or business degree can help, but my own degree is in computer science and business systems, and what got me clients was showing real work, then referrals and trials.

### Can I become a content marketer with no experience?

Yes. Write three evergreen pieces, post them, make a small sample for a real business, and use that as your first portfolio.

### What should I learn first?

Writing in your own voice, a simple post structure like hook, body and call to action, and basic ways to measure what you publish.

### Where do I find my first client?

Start with people and communities you already know: friends with small businesses, college clubs, WhatsApp communities. Show them a sample before you ask for anything.

### How do I keep AI from making my content sound fake?

I tried writing LinkedIn posts with AI and got no engagement. Now I write from my own experience in my own tone.
